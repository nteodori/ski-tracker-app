# Weather & Snow Forecast Reference Guide

This reference provides guidance on evaluating winter weather models, long-range climate indicators, and reputable forecasting platforms for North American and global ski destinations.

---

## 1. Reputable Meteorological & Snow Platforms

| Platform | Primary Strengths | Best Used For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **OpenSnow** | Dedicated mountain micro-climate forecasts, daily snow reports, local meteorologist discussions. | 1-10 day storm totals, seasonal snow previews, resort-specific totals. |
| **NOAA Climate Prediction Center (CPC)** | Official 30-day and 90-day seasonal outlooks for temperature and precipitation anomalies. | Macro seasonal trends, El Niño / La Niña probabilities, jet stream shifts. |
| **OnTheSnow** | Historical snow depth archives, user ratings, live webcams, terrain status. | Comparing year-over-year snow consistency and snowmaking capacity. |
| **Powderchasers** | Deep storm tracking, atmospheric river analysis, backcountry & powder alerts. | Identifying high-conviction powder cycles and storm paths. |
| **Snow-Forecast.com** | Multi-elevation models (top, mid, bottom) worldwide. | Freezing level changes, mountain wind speeds, and rain/snow line evaluation. |

## 2. Long-Range Climate Patterns (ENSO Cycles)

### La Niña Pattern (Cool Phase)
- **Jet Stream Behavior**: Drives the polar jet stream across the Pacific Northwest and Northern Rockies.
- **Regions Favored**:
  - **High Snow Potential**: Pacific Northwest (Mt. Baker, Stevens Pass, Crystal), British Columbia (Whistler, Revelstoke), Northern Rockies (Jackson Hole, Big Sky, Schweitzer, Sun Valley), Northern Utah (Wasatch: Alta, Snowbird, Brighton, Powder Mountain).
  - **Drier / Variable**: Southwest & Southern California (Mammoth variable, Arizona Snowbowl, Taos often drier).

### El Niño Pattern (Warm Phase)
- **Jet Stream Behavior**: Subtropical jet stream strengthens and shifts southward across California and the southern US.
- **Regions Favored**:
  - **High Snow Potential**: Southern Colorado (Telluride, Silverton, Wolf Creek, Crested Butte), New Mexico (Taos), Central & Southern Sierra Nevada (Mammoth Mountain, Palisades Tahoe during heavy atmospheric river cycles), Arizona / Utah border (Brian Head).
  - **Drier / Warmer**: Pacific Northwest and Northern Tier can experience warmer storms and higher rain lines.

### Neutral / Transitional (ENSO-Neutral)
- Storm tracks are typically more variable and dependent on Arctic Oscillations (AO) and Madden-Julian Oscillation (MJO) pulses.
- Intermountain regions with high base elevations (Cottonwood Canyons, Utah; High Rockies, Colorado) provide the highest consistency during neutral years.

## 3. Best Months by Geographic Region

* **Pacific Northwest & Western Canada (Dec - March)**: Huge storm volumes; January/February deliver peak base depth, while March often offers massive spring storms.
* **Utah (Wasatch Front) (Jan - March)**: "Greatest Snow on Earth" due to cold, dry air crossing the Great Salt Lake; peak dry powder in January & February.
* **Colorado High Rockies (Jan - April)**: High base elevations (9,000+ ft) preserve snowpack late into spring; February & March offer the ideal blend of deep coverage and sunny bluebird days.
* **California Sierra Nevada (Jan - March)**: Subject to "Sierra Cement" early, but atmospheric river dumps can drop 5-10+ feet in a single cycle. Late January and February offer peak snowpack.
* **US Northeast (Late Jan - Early March)**: Cold snaps and nor'easters peak in February; relies heavily on snowmaking base in December/January.
