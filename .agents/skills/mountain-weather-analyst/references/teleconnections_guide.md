# Mountain Teleconnections & Snow Meteorology Reference Guide

This guide details the large-scale atmospheric oscillations and thermodynamic principles that dictate North American mountain storm tracks and powder quality.

---

## 1. The Madden-Julian Oscillation (MJO)

The MJO is an eastward-moving wave of tropical convection (Phases 1 through 8) that circles the equator every 30–60 days. It is the primary catalyst for sub-seasonal (2–4 week) storm cycles in the western United States.

| MJO Phase | Primary Convective Location | Downstream Pacific Jet Response | Favored Ski Regions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Phase 1** | Western Hemisphere & Africa | Jet stream retracts toward Asia; Arctic air filters into the Rockies. | Northern Rockies (MT, WY, ID) & Upper Midwest. |
| **Phase 2–3** | Indian Ocean | High-pressure ridge builds along the West Coast; split flow pattern. | Southwest & Southern Rockies (cold/dry). |
| **Phase 4–5** | Maritime Continent (Indonesia) | Strong troughing over the Aleutian Islands; zonal storm track. | Pacific Northwest (WA, OR, BC). |
| **Phase 6** | Western Pacific | East Asian Jet Stream accelerates across the Pacific basin. | Pacific Northwest, Interior BC, Northern Utah. |
| **Phase 7** | Western/Central Pacific | Jet extension toward California; Atmospheric River setup begins. | **Sierra Nevada (CA), Northern Utah (Wasatch), Cascades**. |
| **Phase 8** | Central/Eastern Pacific | Massive subtropical jet extension and deep troughing over Western US. | **Sierra Nevada, Utah, Colorado, Arizona, New Mexico** (Major Powder Cycles). |

## 2. ENSO (El Niño - Southern Oscillation)

Measured by the Oceanic Niño Index (ONI) and Sea Surface Temperature (SST) anomalies in the Niño-3.4 region (5°N–5°S, 120°–170°W).

* **El Niño (SST Anomaly $\ge +0.5^\circ\text{C}$)**:
  - **Mechanisms**: Subtropical jet stream strengthens and remains south.
  - **Snow Impacts**: Heavy atmospheric river storm sequences across the Sierra Nevada, Southern/Central Utah, and Southern Colorado (Telluride, Silverton, Wolf Creek, Taos).
* **La Niña (SST Anomaly $\le -0.5^\circ\text{C}$)**:
  - **Mechanisms**: Polar jet stream dives across the Pacific Northwest and Northern Rockies.
  - **Snow Impacts**: High-frequency cold storms favor Mt. Baker, Whistler, Revelstoke, Jackson Hole, Big Sky, and Northern Utah.

---

## 3. PNA (Pacific-North American) & AO (Arctic Oscillation)

* **PNA Negative ($-$)**: Persistent trough over the Western US and ridge over the Southeast. This is the **ultimate western powder pattern**, funneling storm after storm into the Rockies and Wasatch.
* **PNA Positive ($+$)**: Ridge over the Western US ("Ridiculously Resilient Ridge") deflecting storms north into Alaska or east into the Appalachians.
* **AO Negative ($-$)**: Polar vortex weakens and fractures, allowing dense Arctic air masses to plunge southward into North American ski basins, locking in freezing temperatures and high-elevation snow preservation.

---

## 4. Snow-to-Liquid Ratio (SLR) & The Dendritic Growth Zone (DGZ)

The Snow-to-Liquid Ratio (SLR) defines how many inches of snow accumulate per 1 inch of melted water equivalent:

$$\text{SLR} = \frac{\text{Snow Depth (inches)}}{\text{Snow Water Equivalent (inches)}}$$

### The Dendritic Growth Zone (DGZ)
* **Optimal Temperature**: **$-12^\circ\text{C}$ to $-18^\circ\text{C}$ ($10^\circ\text{F}$ to $0^\circ\text{F}$)** with saturated air and upward vertical velocity ($\omega < 0$).
* Produces stellar dendrites—the delicate 6-sided snowflakes that interlock loosely and trap maximum air volume.

| Ratio | Density ($\text{kg/m}^3$) | Classification | Typical Region & Experience |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **20:1 – 25:1** | $40 - 50$ | **Ultra-Light "Blower / Champagne" Powder** | Steamboat & Cottonwood Canyons (Utah) during cold post-frontal drops. Effortless float. |
| **14:1 – 18:1** | $55 - 70$ | **Classic Dry Powder** | High Rockies (CO, WY, MT) mid-winter standard. Excellent cushioning. |
| **10:1 – 12:1** | $80 - 100$ | **Average / All-Mountain Snow** | Northeast standard & Cascades mid-winter storms. |
| **6:1 – 9:1** | $110 - 165$ | **Dense "Sierra Cement / Cascade Concrete"** | Early storm maritime atmospheric rivers. Unbeatable base-builder. |
